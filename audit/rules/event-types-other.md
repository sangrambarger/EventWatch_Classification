### Military Drills (redirect -> Geopolitical)  _(source: slide 27)_

**Sub-Types:**
- Escalated Military Drills
- Live-Fire Exercises near international waters or sensitive areas
- Aerial Combat and Air Defense Drills near contested airspace or commercial air routes (e.g., Air drills in the Baltic region amid NATO-Russian tensions)
- Naval Blockade Simulations - Exercises focused on controlling maritime access in strategic locations
- Missile Deployment or Testing Drills near international borders
- Nuclear Preparedness Drills
- Rapid Mobilization Drills - rapid deployment of military forces in response to aggressive movements by a neighboring country
- Special Forces Operations Training - Exercises simulating sensitive operations in conflict-prone areas

**Reporting Guidelines (verbatim from source):**
- Do NOT report regular, anticipated exercises with standard maneuvers that occur periodically (e.g., quarterly or annually) and pose no immediate changes to security or supply routes.
- Report in case ofEscalating Military Presence or Border Presence: Increased regional deployment or shift in military readiness by neighboring countries or allies, border invasion during drill, indicating heightened security concerns. Political or Trade Policy Changes: Statements from governments, trade sanctions, or policy shifts related to the region that could disrupt trade flows. Report it under “Geopolitical”

**WarRoom Guidelines (context; not the impact call itself):**
- Geofence the area where drills are directly conducted, plus a surrounding radius of 50 nautical miles (57.5 miles).

**Send Supplier Impact Confirmation?** Generally, we DO NOT issue supplier impact confirmations for military drills. Issue supplier impact confirmation only if The drills have already caused documented disruptions in logistics, such as restricted shipping lanes, increased customs checks, or delays in transport services There’s an official statement or diplomatic action (e.g., sanctions or trade restrictions) that may directly impact the flow of goods in the area

### Mad Cow Disease (redirect -> Regulatory Change / Human Health)  _(source: slide 53)_

**Sub-Types:**
- Beef export ban
- Beef import restriction
- Inter-Country trade implications
- Beef recall due to Mad Cow Disease
- Beef price hyper-increase/hyper-decrease
- Meat Processors/Companies increasing or decreasing beef prices

**Reporting Guidelines (verbatim from source):**
- Panic or emergencies in response to Mad Cow Disease at the city/state/country level
- Beef export ban or import restrictions amid Mad Cow Disease Concerns
- Beef recalls specifically attributed to Mad Cow Disease
- Significant price fluctuations in the beef market caused by Mad Cow Disease-related incidents
- Please select the appropriate event type based on the subject of the news. For example, if a country enforces a ban on beef exports due to Mad Cow Disease, it can be reported as a Regulatory Change. Similarly, if there is a recurring occurrence of Mad Cow Disease cases in cow farms or humans, it can be categorized as Human Health.

**WarRoom Guidelines (context; not the impact call itself):**
- Send impact when a partner is directly involved

**Send Supplier Impact Confirmation?** YES (not for LOW severity; MEDIUM, HIGH, SEVERE only)

### Restricted Access (cross-cutting sourcing rule, not an event type)  _(source: slide 54)_

**Sub-Types:**
- Stories available only on blocked sources

**Reporting Guidelines (verbatim from source):**
- Follow the below criteria when the news or information is exclusively available on a blocked source, with no open-source availability
- Write-up Preparation: Compile a report using the visible information from the blocked news source.
- Event Overview: Use a generic statement at the end of the Event Overview section to highlight the blocked-source information
- If no information is visible on the blocked source, move the story to the "Monitoring" bucket for ongoing tracking
- For Force Majeure- Prepare the write-up using the visible information available, even if it’s only the news headline

**WarRoom Guidelines (context; not the impact call itself):**
- Follow WarRoom guidelines for the respective event type

**Send Supplier Impact Confirmation?** Refer to the relevant Event Type Generic Statement to be used at the end of Event Overview: "Due to limited publicly available data, we are currently unable to confirm all details. While we will continue to monitor and provide updates, we recommend independently verifying whether this event could affect your supply chain."

### Software/Internet Outage (redirect -> Power Outage)  _(source: slide 55)_

**Sub-Types:**
- Internet/data center outages (affecting industrial or service hubs)
- Cloud service disruptions (Microsoft, AWS, Azure, GCP, etc.)
- ERP/CRM/SaaS downtime (SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, etc.)
- Telecom outages hitting enterprise networks
- DNS/CDN routing failures disrupting access
- Fiber cuts or backbone failures

**Reporting Guidelines (verbatim from source):**
- Notify if:
- Mapped/partner site confirms operational impact
- Outage >3 hrs in tech or industrial zones (Taiwan, India, Singapore, etc.)
- Major internet or telecom outage hits multiple mapped locations
- Global/regional SaaS outage affecting ERP/CRM/Logistics platforms
- Confirmed disruption to manufacturing, logistics, or services
- We notify it under Power Outages
- Do NOT notify if:
- Residential/local user issue
- Recovery within 1-2 hrs without business impact
- Services/operations have already resumed
- System outages have recovered

**WarRoom Guidelines (context; not the impact call itself):**
- Geofencing: Polygon or 50 km radius around affected site/region; use separate zones for multi-region outages.
- Create when:
- Mapped site confirms production/service disruption
- Outage lasts >3 hrs with multiple enterprise complaints
- Outage hits industrial zones with multiple mapped sites

**Send Supplier Impact Confirmation?** YES (not for LOW severity; MEDIUM, HIGH, SEVERE only) Trigger when: Site confirms delay in production/services Outage affects mapped suppliers

### Deforestation (redirect -> Forest Fire / Regulatory Change / Environmental Hazard / Protest-Riot)  _(source: slide 56)_

**Sub-Types:**
- Wildfires linked to forest land clearance | New or amended import/export regulations on timber, palm oil, paper, soy, or other commodities
- Smog, haze, or air quality impacts from slash-and-burn deforestation practices | Activist or NGO-led blockades affecting roads, ports, or supplier access
- Government bans/moratoriums on logging or deforestation | Certification (FSC/RSPO) suspensions due to deforestation violations
- Legal rulings, fines, or investigations tied to illegal logging or land clearing

**Reporting Guidelines (verbatim from source):**
- We notify deforestation-related events when:
- Wildfires from deforestation impact supplier sites -> classify under Forest Fire, notify if alerts are issued.
- New deforestation-related import/export regulations are passed -> classify under Regulatory Change, notify immediately.
- Pollution/Smog arising from forest burning -> classify under Environmental Hazard, notify if mapped sites are affected.
- Activist blockades/protests targeting forest roads/logistics -> classify under Protest/Riot, notify if sites or supply chain movement are disrupted.
- Reforestation campaign news -> classify as Not Impactful (NI), avoid notifying unless it directly disrupts mapped operations.
- Note: Only classify "Notify" if thresholds are met for disruption, supplier site proximity, or regulatory enforcement, ensuring relevance.

**WarRoom Guidelines (context; not the impact call itself):**
- Analysts should determine WarRoom creation and supplier impact issuance based on the respective event type's guidelines.
- Follow as per the below event types: Forest Fires -> incase of Wildfires | Regulatory Change -> for forest-based commodities | Environmental Hazard -> from burning forest land | Activist Blockades -> causing logistical/operational disruption.
- Trigger when disruptions are confirmed.

**Send Supplier Impact Confirmation?** YES (not for LOW severity; MEDIUM, HIGH, SEVERE only) Important Note: Deforestation- and forestation-related developments should be classified under existing Event Types based on the nature of the disruption - such as Environmental Hazard, Regulatory Change, Geopolitical, Mine Shutdown, Supply Shortage, or Force Majeure.
